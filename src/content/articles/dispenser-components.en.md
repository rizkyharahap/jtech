The dispenser is the heart of a fuel station. Understanding the components inside it helps
operators decide when a part needs inspecting, cleaning or replacing — which directly affects
how smoothly the station serves customers.

## Main components

**The nozzle** delivers fuel into the vehicle tank. The condition of its seals and cut-off
mechanism determines how safe the filling process is.

**The fuel hose** connects dispenser to nozzle. It works under pressure and is constantly
exposed to fuel, so fine cracks often go unnoticed until they become a leak.

**The solenoid valve** controls fuel flow inside the unit. A weakening valve usually shows up
as an unexplained volume difference.

**The fuel filter** keeps the flow clean and protects the precision components downstream.

**The display and keypad** are the operator's interaction points. A fault here stops the
transaction even when the mechanical parts are healthy.

## A sensible maintenance pattern

Rather than replacing components on guesswork, record two things: volume dispensed and
operator complaints. Both are far more reliable for prioritising replacements than a
calendar schedule alone.
