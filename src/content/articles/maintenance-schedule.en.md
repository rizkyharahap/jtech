Deferred maintenance at a fuel station rarely causes problems at a convenient moment. Here
is an inspection framework you can adapt to your operating volume.

## Daily checks

Things you can see and smell: leaks around the dispenser area, nozzle condition, and
cleanliness of the filling area. This is operator work, not technician work.

## Monthly checks

Volume calibration, hose and coupling condition, and cut-off function. A consistent volume
difference is the earliest signal before a component actually fails.

## Periodic checks

Gradually wearing components — filters, seals, and moving parts inside the unit — are
inspected against volume dispensed rather than against a date.

## Recording matters more than scheduling

A schedule without records tends to become a formality. Record what was checked, what was
found, and what was replaced. Patterns emerge after a few cycles, and replacement decisions
become far more accurate.
